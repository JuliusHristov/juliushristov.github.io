Existence and Mass Gap in Planar SU(N) Yang–Mills Theory on S³ × ℝ and ℝ⁴

Julius Hristov
Leipzig University, Germany

Abstract

The planar large-N limit of Yang–Mills theory suggests a collective description in which color degrees of freedom acquire a geometric realization. We develop a proposed nonperturbative master-field construction for pure SU(N) Yang–Mills theory on S³ × ℝ and investigate its decompactification to ℝ⁴.

Starting from the gauge-reduced fundamental modular region, the large-N matrix algebra is represented by a Poisson algebra on S². The renormalization-group data are organized as period data on the deformed conifold T*S³, leading to the Stenzel geometry and a warped, degree-dependent Riesz structure on a constrained Dolbeault complex. The physical kinetic operator incorporates the ∂-closed condition, Gauss reduction, and a fixed A-period.

Within this framework, we formulate a strict-planar Gaussian master-field construction, analyze a positive spectral threshold and Euclidean reconstruction, and derive radial spectral problems for the scalar-singlet and tensor sectors. Under the proposed glueball-channel identification, the primitive and Lefschetz ground states give the mass ratio M(0−+)/M(0++) ≈ 1.538, close to the large-N lattice estimate.

The construction concerns the strict planar limit. Its identification with Yang–Mills theory and the mathematical assumptions underlying the decompactified physical operator remain important targets for independent verification.

Mathematical framework and computational results

The manuscript studies a proposed geometric master-field formulation of strict planar SU(N) Yang–Mills theory. Its central constructions include a constrained Dolbeault complex on the deformed conifold, an RG-generated warped Riesz metric, a fixed-period physical domain, and the spectral analysis of scalar, pseudoscalar and tensor sectors.

For a technical guide to the mathematical dependencies and outstanding verification questions, see the interactive proof roadmap. Numerical reproduction code and comparisons with lattice results are on the glueball spectrum page.

Research status: This is an independently circulated manuscript presenting a proposed construction, not an independently established resolution of the Yang–Mills mass gap problem. Critical assessment of the operator domains, the collective correspondence, and spectral claims is encouraged.
